# Construction Estimating Excel Case Study

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates a preliminary construction estimating workflow for a fictional commercial office renovation.

The goal was to build an Excel-based estimating model that converts project dimensions into quantity takeoffs, applies material and labor unit costs, develops a preliminary bid, compares subcontractor proposals, and analyzes the project's primary cost drivers.

The workbook was designed as a portfolio case study to demonstrate the application of quantitative analysis and Excel skills to construction estimating.

## Estimating Workflow

The project follows a simplified estimating process:

1. Calculate quantity takeoffs from project dimensions
2. Apply waste factors to estimated quantities
3. Assign material and labor unit costs
4. Calculate estimated material and labor costs
5. Summarize direct project costs
6. Apply contingency, overhead, and profit assumptions
7. Compare subcontractor bids against the internal estimate
8. Evaluate bid completeness, schedule, and exclusions
9. Recommend a best-value subcontractor
10. Analyze major project cost drivers

## Workbook Structure

### Quantity Takeoff
Calculates quantities for flooring, baseboard, and wall surface scopes using project dimensions and waste factors.

### Cost Database
Stores material and labor unit-cost assumptions used throughout the estimating model.

### Estimate Summary
Summarizes material and labor costs by scope and calculates the preliminary estimated bid after contingency, overhead, and profit.

### Bid Comparison
Compares three fictional subcontractor proposals against the internal flooring estimate and evaluates price, schedule, scope completeness, and exclusions.

### Cost Analysis
Analyzes project cost distribution and visualizes material versus labor costs across the major scopes.

## Bid Leveling & Vendor Selection

Three fictional flooring subcontractor bids were evaluated against an internal flooring estimate of **$7,484.40**.

The lowest bid was not automatically selected because it excluded floor preparation and material delivery.

**Gulf Coast Flooring** was recommended as the best-value proposal at **$7,250**, or **$234.40 below the internal estimate**, because the bid provided complete scope coverage while remaining below budget.

This demonstrates the importance of evaluating bid completeness and exclusions in addition to price.

## Cost Analysis

The analysis identified two primary project cost drivers:

- Flooring represents **47.79%** of direct project cost.
- Wall surface work represents **45.51%** of direct project cost.
- Together, these scopes account for **93.30%** of direct project cost.

The material/labor analysis also shows that flooring is more material-intensive, while wall surface work is more labor-intensive.

## Assumptions & Limitations

This is a fictional portfolio case study and is not intended to represent an actual construction bid.

Project quantities, vendors, material costs, labor rates, contingency, overhead, and profit assumptions were created for demonstration purposes. Actual construction estimates would require project drawings, specifications, current supplier and subcontractor pricing, location-specific labor rates, and additional scope validation.
